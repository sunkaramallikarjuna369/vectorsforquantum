Essential Formulas

State Representation

|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩
α, β ∈ ℂ
|α|² + |β|² = 1

Inner Product

⟨φ|ψ⟩ = Σᵢ φᵢ* ψᵢ
⟨ψ|ψ⟩ = ||ψ||²
⟨φ|ψ⟩* = ⟨ψ|φ⟩

Normalization

||ψ|| = √⟨ψ|ψ⟩
|ψ_norm⟩ = |ψ⟩/||ψ||
||ψ_norm|| = 1

Orthogonality

⟨φ|ψ⟩ = 0 → orthogonal
⟨eᵢ|eⱼ⟩ = δᵢⱼ
(orthonormal basis)

Measurement

P(i) = |⟨i|ψ⟩|²
Σᵢ P(i) = 1
(Born rule)

Tensor Product

|ψ⟩ ⊗ |φ⟩ = |ψφ⟩
dim(H₁ ⊗ H₂) = dim(H₁) × dim(H₂)
2ⁿ dimensions for n qubits

Unitary Gates

U†U = UU† = I
||U|ψ⟩|| = ||ψ||
(preserves norm)

Outer Product

|ψ⟩⟨φ| = matrix
Pᵢ = |i⟩⟨i| (projector)
ρ = Σᵢ pᵢ|ψᵢ⟩⟨ψᵢ| (density)

Bloch Sphere

|ψ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + e^(iφ)sin(θ/2)|1⟩
r⃗ = (sin θ cos φ, sin θ sin φ, cos θ)
P(0) = (1+z)/2

Key Quantum Gates

Single-Qubit Gates

Pauli-X (NOT): X = [[0, 1], [1, 0]]
Pauli-Y: Y = [[0, -i], [i, 0]]
Pauli-Z: Z = [[1, 0], [0, -1]]
Hadamard: H = (1/√2)[[1, 1], [1, -1]]
Phase (S): S = [[1, 0], [0, i]]
T Gate: T = [[1, 0], [0, e^(iπ/4)]]

Two-Qubit Gates

CNOT: Controlled-NOT gate
CNOT|00⟩ = |00⟩, CNOT|01⟩ = |01⟩
CNOT|10⟩ = |11⟩, CNOT|11⟩ = |10⟩

SWAP: Exchanges two qubits
CZ: Controlled-Z gate

Important Quantum States

Computational Basis

|0⟩ = [1, 0]ᵀ
|1⟩ = [0, 1]ᵀ

Hadamard Basis

|+⟩ = (1/√2)(|0⟩ + |1⟩)
|−⟩ = (1/√2)(|0⟩ − |1⟩)

Circular Basis

|+i⟩ = (1/√2)(|0⟩ + i|1⟩)
|−i⟩ = (1/√2)(|0⟩ − i|1⟩)

Bell States (Entangled)

|Φ⁺⟩ = (1/√2)(|00⟩ + |11⟩)
|Φ⁻⟩ = (1/√2)(|00⟩ − |11⟩)
|Ψ⁺⟩ = (1/√2)(|01⟩ + |10⟩)
|Ψ⁻⟩ = (1/√2)(|01⟩ − |10⟩)

Key Properties

Vector Space Properties

  • Quantum states form a complex vector space (Hilbert space)
  • Single qubit: 2-dimensional space
  • n qubits: 2ⁿ-dimensional space
  • Inner product defines geometry and measurement
  • Orthonormal bases provide complete state descriptions

Quantum Mechanics Postulates

  • State: Quantum state is a normalized vector in Hilbert space
  • Evolution: Unitary transformations preserve norm
  • Measurement: Born rule gives probabilities from amplitudes
  • Composition: Tensor products for multi-qubit systems

Important Relationships

  • Probability = |amplitude|²
  • Orthogonal states → distinguishable outcomes
  • Superposition → quantum parallelism
  • Entanglement → non-separable states
  • Unitarity → reversible operations

Applications in Quantum Computing

1. Quantum Algorithms

Vectors represent quantum states that evolve through quantum circuits. Algorithms like Shor's and Grover's exploit superposition and interference.

2. Quantum Error Correction

Error correction codes use multi-qubit states and projective measurements to detect and correct errors without destroying quantum information.

3. Quantum Communication

Protocols like quantum teleportation and superdense coding use entangled states and Bell measurements for secure communication.

4. Quantum Simulation

Quantum computers simulate quantum systems by mapping physical states to qubit states and evolution to quantum gates.

5. Quantum Machine Learning

Quantum states encode data, and quantum operations perform computations that may offer advantages for certain ML tasks.
